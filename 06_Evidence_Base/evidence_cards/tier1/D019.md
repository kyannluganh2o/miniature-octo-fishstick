# D019 Tier 1 Evidence Card

## Candidate Claim 1

Claim ID: T1-D019-C01

Candidate claim: At equal leading-shock Mach, detonation and inert-shock cases have similar early wave topology but detonation heat release increases reflected-shock propagation through different postwave velocity and sound-speed states.

Evidence type: primary_numerical

Evidence summary: Matched Mach 4.8 comparison and moving-shock analysis isolate reaction-heat-release effects.

Source locator: LOC-D019-0006

Support type: model_based

Limitations: 2D idealized water droplet and reduced reaction model; project transfer beyond this configuration remains inference.

Relevant parameter IDs: PAR-D019-0005; PAR-D019-0003; PAR-D019-0004

Relevant process/event IDs: PROC-D019-001

Potential chapter role: Detonation-versus-shock loading comparison.

## Candidate Claim 2

Claim ID: T1-D019-C02

Candidate claim: Detonation-induced cavitation collapses sooner and asymmetrically because the low-pressure region behind the detonation wave persists for less time than behind the inert shock.

Evidence type: primary_numerical

Evidence summary: Coupled phase-transition simulations compare full vapor-zone growth and collapse cycles.

Source locator: LOC-D019-0007

Support type: simulation_resolved

Limitations: Cavitation model is homogeneous; thermal conduction is omitted for main-droplet timescale separation.

Relevant parameter IDs: PAR-D019-0015; PAR-D019-0016

Relevant process/event IDs: PROC-D019-002; HIST-D019-001

Potential chapter role: Phase-transition timescale mechanism.

## Candidate Claim 3

Claim ID: T1-D019-C03

Candidate claim: Lower detonation postwave velocity suppresses the shock-case RT-driven forward jet and instead promotes leeward flattening through the recirculation-zone vortex.
