I. INTRODUCTION
When liquid drops are suddenly exposed to high speed
gas ﬂows, they deform and shatter, which is a phenomenon
/H20849aerobreakup/H20850that is known to intensify with increasing We-
ber number /H20849We, expressing the ratio of inertia to surface
tension forces /H20850. This progression is known to be marked by
transitions in morphology, the so-called breakup regimes,
and correspondingly by /H20849critical/H20850Weber numbers that are
intended to quantify the onsets of these transitions. The re-
gimes of aerobreakup are important not only in that they
embody the governing physics but also in that they deﬁne the
quantitative features of the resulting particle cloud /H20849dimen-
sions, effective density, size distribution /H20850and, thus, its dis-
persal and/or mixing characteristics. Such processes are the
fundamental component in a great variety of practical appli-
cations including internal combustion or pulse-detonation
engines, nuclear fusion devices, agricultural and environ-
mental sprayings, and, as of recently, certain defense appli-
cations that involve the explosive dissemination and/or atmo-
spheric dispersal of liquid agents released at supersonic
speeds.
