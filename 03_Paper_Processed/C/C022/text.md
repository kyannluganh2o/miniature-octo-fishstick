ABSTRACT  
The droplet breakup behaviors under the action of shock wave are frequently observed in liquid-fueled deto -
nation-based engines. In this work, the secondary droplet size distribution after the breakup of a sub-milimeter 
droplet under high velocity cross flow conditions (the gas flow velocity is 105.64 m/s) was experimentally 
determined on a horizontal shock tube with the developed digital in-line holography technique. During the 
droplet breakup process, the spatial (two-dimensional, three-dimensional) and droplet size distributions of two 
characteristic stages (early and late stages) were majorly concerned based on the reconstructed droplet cloud 
field. At the early stage, the liquid-mist field appeared as the barrel shape, and the probability distribution of the 
droplet number in the cross-stream direction presented as “ M shape ” . While the secondary droplet cloud field 
appeared as the conical shape, and the probability distribution presented as the parabolic shape at the late stage. 
Both the probability distribution of the droplet number along the flow direction increased firstly then decreased 
at the early and late stages with the increasing of distance. In addition, the peak position of the droplet number 
probability distribution also changed with time. The secondary droplets mainly distributed in the range of 0~20 
μ m for the early and late stages, and its number accounted for more than 93%. Meanwhile, the corresponding 
volume probabilities changed from 12% to 72%, due to the consistent stripping process. In addition, the droplet 
size distribution could be well described by the log-normal and root-normal distribution curves at both stages. 
The study of the spatial and droplet size distributions is believed to be of importance for corresponding droplet 
breakup modeling.   
1. Introduction 
The detonation engine is one of most promising propulsion ap -
proaches in the aviation area due to its simple structure and high ther -
mal efficiency. This kind of engine is powered by the detonation wave, 
which consists of the leading-shock wave and the reaction region. The 
shock wave-droplet interaction phenomena are frequently observed in 
the liquid-fueled detonation-based engines ( Li et al., 2011 ): The 
leading-shock wave sweeps the pre-filled fuel mist, and the shock 
wave-droplet interaction is then inevitable in this engine. Understanding 
the breakup behaviors arisen from the shock wave interacting with the 
droplet favors the mixture formation enhancement and ignition system 
design for the detonation engines. 
From a fundamental point of view, when the shock wave passes 
through the droplet, the droplet experiences deformation and breakup 
process immediately under the action of the stable gas flow induced by 
the shock wave. The generated droplets mist from their parent directly 
affects the downstream mixing, ignition and combustion process, which 
finally influences the engine performance ( Guildenbecher et al., 2009 ).

velocity between the droplet and the flow, as characterized by the Weber

The droplet size and velocity distributions of bag and sheet-thinning

2. Experimental specifications 
2.1. Experimental setup 
The schematic diagram of the experimental system is shown in Fig. 1 , 
which consists of the shock tube, the droplet generator, and the holo -
graphic detection system. The shock tube is used to generate the shock 
wave, through which the required high-speed flow conditions are ob -
tained. The droplet diameter is about 0.2 mm, which is of the same order 
of primary droplet size from typical injection system. The detailed 
introduction of the shock tube, and the pneumatic droplet generator can 
be found in our previous work( Shen et al., 2019 ). 
The DIH mainly consists of a laser, the beam adjustment device, the 
optical lens group, and a CCD camera. Specifically, the laser wavelength 
is 532 nm, the beam diameter is 7 mm, the power of the single pulse is 
150 mJ, and the pulse width is 6 ns (the maximum pulse power is 200 
mJ, the range of pulse width is 6~8 ns). The beam adjustment device is 
used to adjust the light beam and output plane wave of high quality, 
which consists of a spatial filter, a mirror and a beam expander. With 
adjustment, the final output beam diameter is 100 mm. The optical lens 
group is located between the shock wave tube and the CCD camera, 
which is employed to ensure enough diffraction order such that fringes 
can be recorded by the CCD camera. In addition, with the amplification 
of the optical lens group, the spatial resolution is increased to by 2.4 μ m/ 
pixel.

object wave ( O ) and the reference wave ( R ). These two waves interfere 
with each other on the CCD surface and form interference fringes, which 
are recorded as digital information by the CCD camera. Fig. 2 (b) shows 
the schematic diagram of the interference process, and the light intensity 
I is determined by

2.3. Operation sequence and experimental conditions 
The droplet breakup process is captured with the method of long- 
exposure mode, during which two laser pulses pass through the liquid- 
mist successively. The first pulse is used to capture the initial state of 
the droplet, including the droplet diameter and position; the second 
pulse is used to capture the detailed information of the flow field. The 
time interval of two pulses is regulated by a synchronization device 
DG535.

3.3. Droplet identification and boundary location 
After image reconstruction, secondary droplets at different depth of 
field are identified. However, as aforementioned, secondary droplets are 
out of focus at different levels when their depth of field changes. The 
droplet boundary can not be identified precisely. As such, the droplet 
boundary determination algorithm is required. 
The operation of depth-of-field extension is to determine the droplet 
boundary, which is achieved by making secondary droplets of different 
positions to a pseudo focal plane to such that to obtain a clear in-focus 
image. The wavelet decomposition method is adopted to obtain the 
depth-of-field extended image, the specific steps of wavelet decompo -
sition can be found in Ref. ( Wu et al., 2014 ). In addition, as shown in 
Fig. 5 , there is a large gray value gradient between the gas-liquid 
boundary when the droplet is focused. Therefore, the droplet bound -
aries can be identified by the gray value gradient. The detailed pro -
cedures can be acquired in Lebrun ’ s work ( Lebrun et al., 2003 ).

4. The spatial distribution of the liquid-mist field 
4.1. The spatial distribution at the early stage (T = 1.50) 
4.1.1. Validation of the reconstructed result 
Fig. 6 compares the initial holographic image and the corresponding 
reconstructed x-y plane image (the initial holographic image can be 
regarded as the traditional shadow image). It shows that the primary 
characteristics are well captured, such as the piercing behavior and the 
unbroken droplet. These common characteristics can verify the cor -
rectness of the reconstruction method to some extent. In addition, there 
are some droplets on the edge of the liquid-mist field on the recon -
structed 2-D image ( Fig. 6 (b)), which cannot be observed clearly on the 
initial holographic image ( Fig. 6 (a)). This feature shows the advantages 
of the holographic technology. 
It is pointed out that the y -axis direction is along the flow direction, 
and its the coordinate origin is the front stagnation point of the droplet. 
The z -axis direction is perpendicular to the CCD camera plane, and its 
coordinate origin is on the boundary of the reconstruction area. The x - 
axis is perpendicular to the y-z plane, and its coordinate origin is also on 
the boundary of the holographic image. 
4.1.2. The spatial distribution 
The spatial distribution of the secondary droplets at the early stage ( t 
= 0.0657 ms) is shown in Fig. 7 (a). It can be found that the liquid-mist 
diffuses along the downstream/vertical airflow directions with a several 
large droplets remained in the center, and the liquid-mist field basically 
appears as a barrel shape. The sub-droplets are gathered near the large 
droplets. 
The spatial distribution of the liquid-mist field is closely related to 
the droplet size. To clearly show the spatial distribution, the liquid-mist 
fields of different size ranges are demonstrated in Fig. 7 (b)-(d) respec -
tively.  It can be seen that when droplet diameter is smaller than 10 μ m, 
the corresponding spatial distribution is rather dispersed, the liquid-mist 
has diffused to the whole reconstruction area. When droplet diameter is 
in the range of 10 μ m~20 μ m, the corresponding spatial distribution 
along the flow direction has decreased obviously, which is mainly 
concentrated in regions between y = 1500 μ m and 3000 μ m. What ’ s 
more, with the further increasing of the droplet size( d > 20 μ m), the 
corresponding spatial distribution has further decreased, and the son 
droplets are mainly concentrated around the unbroken core droplet. 
Upon the shock wave passing through the droplet, secondary drop -
lets are stripped from the parent droplet consistently. The captured 
liquid-mist field is the cumulative distribution of the stripping process. 
Moreover, droplets of smaller size are easily to adjust the trajectories to 
flow, which leads to the fact that the gap of the spatial displacement is 
aggravated as time evolutes. As a result, the spatial distribution area 
decreases with the increase of droplet size. In addition, when sub- 
droplet diameter is larger than 20 μ m, droplets continue to experi -
ences further breakup after been stripped from the core droplet, which 
also affects the spatial distribution.

from the center. However, with the further increase of the distance, the 
droplet diffusion ability decreases. These two factors make the droplet 
number probability distribution decrease again after reaching the

In addition, the droplet number probability distribution is also 
related to the droplet size. It can be seen from Fig. 8 (a) that when droplet 
diameter is smaller than 10 μ m, the corresponding probability distri -

probability distribution. For larger droplets ( d > 10 μ m), obvious regu -
larity cannot be observed. These changes are related to piercing be -
haviors (demonstrated in Fig. 6 ).  When R-T waves break, droplets of 
large size will be generated and droplet diffusing direction changes. As a 
result, the distribution of droplets ( d > 10 μ m) shows little regularity. 
The droplet number probability distribution along the y-axis at the 
early stage ( t = 0.0657 ms) is demonstrated in Fig. 8 (b). With the 
increasing of the distance, the droplet number probability firstly in -
creases then decreases, and reaches its peak at y ≈ 1450 μ m. It is 
considered that in this region y < 1450 μ m, small droplets are 
continuously stripped from the parent droplet, which leads to the in -
crease of the probability along the y direction. With the further increase 
of distance, the liquid-mist field becomes dispersed under the action of 
the droplet size effect (small droplet moves faster than large droplet) and 
the spatial and temporal cumulative effects. 
It is also noted from Fig. 8 (b) that the variation trend of different 
droplet sizes all increases firstly and then decreases. However, due to the 
difference of diffusion ability, the peak position changes with the droplet 
size. 
The droplet number probability distribution along the z-axis at the 
early stage ( t = 0.0657 ms) is presented in Fig. 8 (c). The droplet number 
probability distribution is basically the same as that of Fig. 8 (a). This is 
reasonable since the breakup process can be regarded as an axisym -
metric phenomenon, x /z-axes are only different from each other in 
terms of mathematical statistics. However, the specific distribution is 
different because the breakup process is also disturbed by many factors 
such as initial disturbance.

The spatial distribution of the liquid-mist field changes with time.

4.2.3. The spatial distribution regulation 
The spatial distribution of the liquid-mist field changes with time. 
Fig. 11 (a) shows the droplet number probability distribution along the 
x -axis at the late stage. It firstly increases then decreases along the x axis 
direction and peaks at x = 1.0 mm (central position), which implies that 
the generated droplets are mainly distributed in the center of the liquid- 
mist field. It is believed that the consistent stripping process has led to 
this distribution. 
The comparison between the early and the late stage secondary 
droplet distributions indicates that, the stripped droplets are with high 
diffusion ability at the early stage, which makes the probability distri -
bution present as the “ M shape ” . In addition, the diffusion ability of the 
stripped droplets decreases with time, which leads to the gathering of 
the droplets at the center of the liquid-mist field. Furthermore, under the 
action of the temporal and spatial cumulative effects (the distribution of 
the liquid mist at certain instant is the gathering of the stripped drop -
lets), the stripped droplets diffuse outwards and move out of the 
reconstruction area. As a result, under the action of these two factors, 
the probability distribution presents as the “ parabolic shape ” . 
Moreover, the droplet number probability distribution at the late 
stage is also related to the droplet size. As shown in Fig. 11 (a), the 
corresponding probability distribution remains basically unchanged

( d > 10 μ m), the droplet number probability distribution basically pre -
sents as the parabolic shape. This is because the distribution of small 
droplet ( d < 10 μ m) is relatively uniform after a long period of time, while 
the distribution of large droplet ( d > 20 μ m) is still affected by the 
stripping process.

It is also noted that the droplet number probability distribution of

6. Conclusions 
To investigate the spatial and droplet size distributions of the liquid- 
mist field, droplet breakup experiments were carried out on a horizontal 
shock tube. Results showed that: 
The holographic image was captured with a high spatial resolution, 
2.4 μ m/pixel. The 3-D spatial distribution was also reconstructed suc -
cessfully with the developed algorithm, which can help understand the 
breakup process and offer the fundamental experimental data for the 
corresponding numerical simulation and theoretical calculation. 
The shape of liquid-mist field changed with time. The liquid-mist 
field presented as the barrel shape at the early stage of breakup, but 
appeared as the conical shape at the late stage. With time evolution, the 
droplet number probability distribution along the cross-stream direction 
(x/z axis) changed from the “ M shape ” to the “ parabolic shape ” . The 
change of the diffusion ability and the temporal and spatial cumulative 
effects jointly resulted in this variation. The droplet number probability 
distribution along the flow direction both increased firstly, and then 
decreased with distance at the early and late stage. However, the peak 
position of the droplet number probability at the late stage was further 
away from the head of the liquid-mist field. This is because the broken 
droplets were experiencing an acceleration motion, and the son-droplet 
spacing would increase with time. 
Both the droplet diameter and volume probability distributions 
varied with time. The droplet diameters mainly distributed in the range 
of 0~20 μ m at the early and late stage, and the corresponding droplet 
number accounted for 94% and 93% respectively. Meanwhile, the cor -
responding volume probabilities changed from 12% to 72%, due to the 
persistent stripping process.
