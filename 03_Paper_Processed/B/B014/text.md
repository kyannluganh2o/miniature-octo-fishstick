abstract
The microscopic characteristics of hydrogen jet affect the concentration distribution and
gas-air mixing of high-pressure gas jets for a hydrogen engine. In this study, the schlieren
method was used to record the hydrogen jet for an outward-opening injector. The results
showed that the jet asymmetricity was large in the initial stage of the jet development,
then decreased rapidly as the jet developed, and ﬁnally entered a stable range of 13% e38%
when the valve fully opened. The increase of the ambient pressure was beneﬁcial to reduce
the ﬂuctuation range of the asymmetricity. The steady S of an outward-opening injector
(S ¼ 0.7 ± 0.03) is larger than that of a single-hole injector ( S ¼ 0.25 ± 0.05). The increase in
injection pressure and the decrease in ambient pressure caused S to decrease to 1 faster.
The steady G of an outward-opening injector was experimentally determined, and the
value ( G ¼ 0.98 ± 0.1) was smaller than that of a single-hole injector. According to the
transient G, the injection process can be divided into the developing phase and the self-
similar phase. G increased rapidly with time and then stabilized after the injector valve
reached its maximum lift position. The gas concentration distribution was uneven along
the axial direction. The mole fraction decreased with the increase in axial penetration, and
entered a stable range of 0.27 e0.37 when axial penetration was greater than 20 mm. The
average equivalent ratio was large at the beginning of injection under different pressure
ratios, and then decreased and ﬁnally stabilized in the range of 1.4 e5.

Introduction
Due to the depletion of fossil resources and strict emission
regulations, scholars have performed extensive studies
regarding diesel spray and combustion to achieve low emis-
sions and efﬁcient combustion in diesel engines [ 1e3]. Clean
fuels, such as natural gas and hydrogen, produce less harmful
emissions during combustion. Moreover, gas engines can use
a higher compression ratio, enabling higher thermal efﬁ-
ciencies at full loads. Therefore, gas engines fueled with nat-
ural gas and hydrogen have been developed rapidly. Most
scholars have focused on the combustion characteristics of
gas engines and obtained valuable information [ 4,5]. The jet
characteristics directly determine the mixing effects of gas
and air, which affect the combustion characteristics of gas
engines. Therefore, research on the jet characteristics of
gaseous fuel is particularly important.
Xie et al. [ 6] conducted a visualization investigation on
high-pressure hydrogen jets. They reported that the penetra-
tion rate was high at the start of injection but decreased
gradually as time progressed. Deng et al. [ 7] experimentally
investigated the mixing characteristics of high-pressure
hydrogen and oxygen jets in an argon atmosphere. Their re-
sults indicated that hydrogen jet entrained more total argon
volume for its higher penetration and dispersion angle.
Hamzehloo et al. [ 8,9] performed a numerical study on
underexpanded hydrogen and natural gas jets. They found
that the difference between hydrogen and natural gas jets in
terms of penetration originated from differences in both sonic
characteristics and diffusivity. Li et al. [ 10] reported uneven
hydrogen distributions in radial and axial directions during
injection, and that the concentration distributions differed.
The effect of injection timing on the formation of a
hydrogeneair mixture was investigated by Huang et al. [ 11].
They reported that the injection timing signiﬁcantly affected
the mixing effects of the gas eair mixture and the uniformity
of the concentration distribution, and the microscopic char-
acteristics of a high-pressure gas jet determined the com-
bustion process of a gas engine. Additionally, scholars have
investigated the jet characteristics of natural gas. Ouellette
[12] conducted an early research on natural gas jet. The
schlieren method and particle image velocimetry (PIV) were
used to study the effects of pressure ratio (PR) and injection
duration on gas jet, and a formula for predicting the pene-
tration of underexpanded gas jets was proposed. Zhao et al.
[13] reported that the effect of ambient pressure P
amb on
penetration was signiﬁcantly greater than that of the injection
pressure P
inj, and that ambient pressure determined the effect
of injection pressure on penetration. Erfan et al. [ 14] investi-
gated the injection characteristics of natural gas jets injected
using a single-hole nozzle direct injector and presented the
correlation of tip penetration for the near and far ﬁelds of the
jet. Hajialimohammadi et al. [ 15,16] studied the effects of in-
jection and ambient pressures on jet macroscopic character-
istics. They discovered that tip penetration was sensitive to
the nozzle PR, and new correlations for tip speed and tip
penetration were presented. Sankesh et al. [ 17] used a visu-
alization method to study highly underexpanded transient jet
ﬂows. Their results showed that the increase in PR imposed a
weak effect on the jet macroscopic characteristics when PR
was large. Dong et al. [ 18] experimentally evaluated the
microscopic characteristics of natural gas jets for a gas
injector with various nozzle structures. It was discovered that
the air entrainment effect and the average equivalent ratio
were not signiﬁcantly affected by the change of injection
pressure and oriﬁce diameter.
A majority of the studies mentioned above focused on the
macroscopic characteristics of gas jets. The research on
microscopic characteristics of gas jets were less reported,
especially the self-similarity characteristics and the nonuni-
formity of gas concentration distribution. Additionally,
research on the jet characteristics is important for gas en-
gines, and scholars gave more attention to the jet character-
istics of natural gas. However, the jet characteristics of
hydrogen have been less studied. In this study, the schlieren
method was used to experimentally investigate the high-
pressure injection process of hydrogen in a constant-volume
vessel (CVV). The microscopic characteristics of hydrogen
jets under different PRs were revealed, which provided basic
theoretical support for optimizing gas engines.
Experimental system
Experimental apparatus
The density of gaseous fuel is much lower than that of liquid
fuel. A high-speed camera cannot directly capture the high-
quality images of a gas injection process. Therefore, the
schlieren method was used to obtain images of gas injection
in this study. The schlieren method is based on the principle
that gradient changes in the gas refractive index cause optical
inhomogeneity. The detailed information of the high-speed
jet ﬂow ﬁeld can be obtained by the density change of the
ﬂuid. As shown in Fig. 1 , the experimental apparatus com-
prises an injection system and an optical system. The injec-
tion system is primarily composed of two high pressure tanks
with regulators, pressure gauge, CVV, and controller. Nitrogen
was selected in the experiment because it is an inert gas to
ensure the safety of the experiment, and the molecular weight
of nitrogen is close to that of air which avoids large errors
when calculating the mass of ambient gas in the jet. The op-
tical system is arranged in a “Z” shape. Light from a light
source passed through a slit, rendering it a high-energy light
source. The light was reﬂected by a concave mirror, forming a
parallel light beam of diameter 100 mm. The parallel light
passed through the glass of the CVV, and subsequently
through a concave mirror, plane mirror, and knife edge on the
other side of the CVV. Information regarding the gas jet was
captured by a high-speed camera. To obtain high-quality gas
jet schlieren images, this high-speed camera was set to a
resolution of 416 /C2 400 pixels, a shooting frequency of 17, 241
frames per second, and an exposure time of 30 ms. The
controller contains a synchronous trigger function that en-
ables the injection time of the gas injector valve to be
consistent with the photograph capture time of the high-
speed camera. Table 1 presents the experimental conditions.
The experiment was repeated ﬁve times to reduce errors, and
the average value was obtained.

Deﬁnition of jet parameters
Jet penetration
In most previous studies, the deﬁnition of jet penetration is
based on a single-hole nozzle, and the premise of this deﬁni-
tion is that the jet development of both sides is the same.
However, the outward-opening nozzle causes the jet devel-
opment of the left and right sides to differ. Therefore, the
deﬁnition of jet penetration for a single-hole nozzle is not
suitable for an outward-opening nozzle. As shown in Fig. 2 ,
Sankesh et al. [ 17] proposed a deﬁnition method of jet pene-
tration which is applicable for an outward-opening nozzle.
The radial penetration D
1 and D2 are deﬁned as the maximum
distance from the injector axis to the maximum-width loca-
tion of the gas jet perpendicular to the injector axis. The radial
penetration D is the sum of D
1 and D2. The axial penetration
Zt1 and Zt2 are deﬁned as the distance from the nozzle opening
to the tip of the gas jet along the injector axis. The axial
penetration Z
t is the mean of Zt1 and Zt2.

Results and discussion
For a single-hole gas injector, the jet is mainly developed in
the axial direction, so the evaluation of the axial penetration is
important. Gas engines require large amounts of fuel for
actual work, and the single-hole injectors cannot be applied to
gas engines due to insufﬁcient mass ﬂow rate. Therefore, the
outward-opening injectors are typically used to achieve high
pressure injection of gas engines. Fig. 4 shows a sequence of
schlieren images depicting the development of hydrogen jet at
ambient pressure of 5 bar and injection pressure of 14 bar. As
shown, the radial penetration and axial penetration both
increased with the injection time. However, the degree of jet
development in the radial and axial directions differed due to
the structure of the outward-opening injector. With the jet
development, jet surface started to generate some wrinkles,
and the local gas was separated from the jet body. In addition,
the development in the radial direction was faster than that in
the axial direction at the initial stage of jet development.
Then, the jet developed fast in the axial direction. The devel-
opment process of gas jets can be observed intuitively from
the schlieren images, and the qualitative law can be obtained.
However, the microscopic characteristics of gas jets cannot be
evaluated quantitatively. Therefore, in this study, the
schlieren images obtained experimentally were morphologi-
cally processed, and the microscopic characteristics were
analyzed and discussed.
The jet development in the radial direction
As shown in Fig. 5 , the radial penetration under different in-
jection pressures and ambient pressures increased almost
linearly with time at the early stage of jet development
(t ¼ 0e0.52 ms). After t ¼ 0.52 ms, the radial penetration under
different injection conditions continued to increase with time,
and this increase declined gradually. The radial penetration
increased with an increase in the injection pressure at the
same ambient pressures. For example, when the ambient
pressure was 3 bar, at the injection time of 1 ms, the axial
penetration increased from 56 to 67 mm when the injection
pressure increased from 10 to 18 bar. Under the same injection
pressures, the increase in ambient pressure led to a decrease
in the radial penetration. This phenomenon occurred because
the density of the ambient gas increased as the jet developed,
and the ambient gas compressed the jet in the radial direction.
The jet development in the axial direction
Fig. 6 shows the effect of injection pressure on axial pene-
tration. It can be seen that axial penetration increased with
the increase in injection pressure and the decrease in ambient
pressure. The axial penetration at different injection condi-
tions increased linearly with different proportions in the two
stages. The axial penetration curves under different injection
pressures and ambient pressures coincided at the stage of
t ¼ 0e0.52 ms, and the curves increased linearly with the in-
jection time. When t > 0.52 ms, although the degree of in-
crease in the axial penetration was different under different
injection conditions, all of these curves increased linearly

Jet asymmetricity of hydrogen jet
It can be seen from Fig. 4 that the formation and evolution of
vortex was not symmetrical along the axial direction. In order
to quantitatively analyze the asymmetry of hydrogen jet for
an outward-opening injector, the jet asymmetricity was
deﬁned as the ratio of the volume difference between left and
right sides of the gas jet ( V
letf - Vright) to the mean jet volume
(VJet/2). This deﬁnition took into account the axial and radial
growth of the gas jet [ 17]. As shown in Fig. 7 , the jet asym-
metricity was large (close to 100%) in the initial stage of the jet
development, and even greater than 100% under some con-
ditions. At the stage of t ¼ 0e0.52 ms, the jet asymmetricity
decreased rapidly as injection time progressed. When
t > 0.52 ms, the jet asymmetricity entered a stable range of
13%e38%, achieving dynamic stability. The reason for the
above phenomenon was that the development of hydrogen jet
in the axial direction dominated at the initial stage of jet
development, and the left side of the jet developed faster than
the right side. These two factors caused the jet asymmetricity
to be larger. Over time, the increase in radial penetration
declined (as shown in Fig. 5 ), and the axial penetration
increased rapidly (as shown in Fig. 6 ), thereby the jet asym-
metricity started to enter a stable range. Additionally, it can be
seen that the value of jet asymmetricity was positive under all
experimental conditions, which indicated that the jet devel-
opment of the left side was more sufﬁcient than that of the
right side. Overall, the increase in ambient pressure was
beneﬁcial to reduce the ﬂuctuation range of the jet
asymmetricity.

Self-similarity characteristics of hydrogen jet
In the study of gas jet, self-similarity characteristics can be
evaluated by the ratio of the radial penetration D to the axial
penetration Zt. When the ratio is stable, it indicates that the
jets reach a self-similarity. This ratio is deﬁned as the self-
similarity index S. Rizk [ 23] measured the penetration rate of
incompressible natural gas jets. He found that S ¼ 0.25 ± 0.05
when the jets reached a self-similarity. Hajialimohammadi
et al. [ 24] experimentally determined that the S of natural gas
jet was 0.33 ± 0.05 at d ¼ 0.4e0.6 mm and PR ¼ 1.5e4. Addi-
tionally, it was found that smaller diameter of nozzle hole
gave higher S values, while nozzle hole diameter did not affect
it signiﬁcantly for different PRs. Hajialimohammadi et al. [ 25]
further investigated the self-similarity characteristics of he-
lium jet, and determined that S ¼ 0.4e0.48 at d ¼ 0.8 mm and
PR ¼ 2e5. Sankesh et al. [ 17] performed a study on the gas jet
for an outward-opening injector, and S was determined to be
1.36e1.72. In his study, D was always greater than Z
t due to the
entire injection process did not record by the schlieren
images.
A majority of the studies mentioned above focused on the
gas jet for a single-hole injector. However, research on the
self-similarity characteristics of an outward-opening injector
applied on gas engines were rare. As shown in Fig. 8 , for an
outward-opening injector, the axial penetration was small at
the initial stage of jet development, and the jet developed
faster in the radial direction. Therefore, S was much larger
than 1 (close to 3.5) in the initial stage of the jet development.
Over time, the increase in radial penetration declined and the
axial penetration continued to increase. S decreased rapidly
and started to be smaller than 1 at a certain moment. With the
further development of the jet, S stabilized in a range of
0.7 ± 0.03 (as marked in the ﬁgure), which indicated that the
jets reached a self-similarity. There were similarities and
differences between the variation laws of an outward-opening
injector in this study and that of a single-hole injector. The
similarity was that S decreased from a large value with time,
and ﬁnally stabilized in a certain range. There were also some
differences. For an outward-opening injector, the differences
of jet development in radial and axial directions made S
greater than 1 at the initial stage of jet development, and then
S was smaller than 1 after a period of time. For a single-hole
injector, S was smaller than 1 during the entire injection
process owing to the axial penetration was always greater
than the radial penetration [ 24]. At the same ambient pres-
sure, the increase in injection pressure caused S to decrease to
1 faster, thereby achieving a more sufﬁcient development in
the axial direction than in the radial direction. For example, at
the ambient pressure of 3 bar, injection pressure decreased
from 18 to 10 bar, and the moment of S ¼ 1 was delayed by
0.6 ms. The increase in ambient pressure made the decrease
in S slowed, and resulting in a larger steady S when the jets
reach a self-similarity. For example, at the injection pressure
of 10 bar, S were 0.67 and 0.73 when the ambient pressures
were 3 and 5 bar. Overall, S was slightly affected by the
ambient pressure and injection pressure, and this conclusion
was consistent with Sankesh 's[ 17]. A larger S means that D is
greater than Z
t, and the corresponding penetration constant G
is smaller. The relationship between S and G was discussed in
the later sections.
Penetration constant of hydrogen jet
The penetration constant G is an important parameter for
accurately predicting the axial penetration using Eq. (2), and
this value is usually determined by experimental results.
Outllette and Hill [ 19] experimentally found that the G of
natural gas jet was 3.0 ± 0.1 at d ¼ 0.5 mm and PR ¼ 1.5e5.3.
Rogers et al. [ 26] investigated the G of natural gas jet for
d ¼ 1 mm and PR up to 400, and G was determined to be 3.19.
Some scholars performed experimental research on G of he-
lium jet at d ¼ 0.5e1.0 mm and PR ¼ 1.5e36. It was found that G
in the range of 1.89 e2.95 [ 27e29]. It can be seen from the
aforementioned research that G under different conditions
was not same, and the differences may be affected by the
nozzle hole diameter, PR and the accuracy of experimental
results. Overall, the value of G is about 3 for a single-hole
injector. As shown in Fig. 10 , for an outward-opening
injector, G increased rapidly in the stage of t ¼ 0e0.52 ms for
different pressure ratios. G continued to increase with time at
the stage of t ¼ 0.52e1.5 ms, and this increase declined grad-
ually. When t > 0.52 ms, G stabilized in a range of 0.98 ± 0.1.
According to the value of G, the injection process can be
divided into two phases: developing phase and self-similar
phase. The stage of t ¼ 0e0.52 ms was the opening phase of
the injector valve and G increased linearly. The injector valve
reached its maximum lift position at t
¼ 0.52 ms. After
t ¼ 0.52 ms, the increase of G gradually decreased. The jets
reached a self-similarity at t ¼ 1.5 ms, and G was stable within
a certain value. Additionally, the effect of PR on the steady G
was not signiﬁcantly, and G will stabilize at a certain value
under different PRs. The steady G of an outward-opening
injector was smaller than that of a single-hole injector. The
reason was that S of an outward-opening injector was usually
larger than that of a single-hole injector. For example, S was
0.7 ± 0.03 in this study, and other scholars’ value were 0.2e0.48
[23,24,27].

Mixing characteristics of hydrogen jet
The average equivalent ratio f affects the combustion and
emission characteristics of gas engines. As shown in Fig. 11, f
was large at the beginning of injection under different injec-
tion pressures and ambient pressures. Over time (within
0e1 ms), f decreased rapidly owing to the increase of air
entrainment effect with the jet development. After t ¼ 1m s ,f
stabilized within a range of 1.4 e5. The average equivalent
ratio f decreased with the increase in PR, and the effect was
not pronounced. This indicated that it was not effective for a
gas engine to improve the mixing effect simply by changing
the injection pressure. It was different from the high-pressure
injection of the liquid fuel engines. Therefore, strong air tur-
bulence was needed in order to improve the mixing effect for
hydrogen engine.
Gas concentration of hydrogen jet
Fig. 12 shows the mole fraction of hydrogen jet along the axial
direction at different PRs. As shown, the variation laws of
hydrogen jet were similar under different injection condi-
tions. The mole fraction was large (close to 1) at the beginning
of injection process. This was because the jet volume was
small at the initial stage of jet development, and the gas
accounted for the main part of the jet. In the near-ﬁeld region
of the jet ( Z
t < 20 mm), the mole fraction decreased with the
increase in Zt, which indicated that the hydrogen concentra-
tion gradually decreased. The aforementioned phenomenon
occurred because jet developed faster in the axial direction
over time, and more air was introduced into the jet farther
from the nozzle hole, resulting in a lower gas concentration.
Mole fraction entered a stable range of 0.27 e0.37 when Z
t was
greater than 20 mm. The effects of injection pressure and
ambient pressure on gas concentration was insigniﬁcant, but
the increase of the ambient pressure caused the gas concen-
tration of hydrogen jet along the axial direction to increase
slightly.

Conclusions
In this study, the schlieren method was used to record the
injection process of hydrogen and a quantitative study was
carried out. The microscopic characteristics of gas jet under
different PRs were obtained as follows:
(1) The radial and axial penetrations both increased with
the increase in injection pressure and the decrease in
ambient pressure. The increase of the ambient pressure
was beneﬁcial to reduce the ﬂuctuation range of the
asymmetricity of the hydrogen jet. Under different PRs,
the jet asymmetricity was large in the initial stage of the
jet development, then decreased rapidly as the jet
developed, and ﬁnally reached stability.
(2) For an outward-opening injector, S was larger than 1 in
the initial stage of the jet development and subse-
quently decreased to 1 and ﬁnally stabilized under
different PRs. The increase in injection pressure and the
decrease in ambient pressure caused a more sufﬁcient
development in the axial direction than in the radial
direction. The injection and ambient pressures slightly
affected the steady S, but affected the transient S (from
S > 1t o S < 1).
(3) The steady G of an outward-opening injector was
experimentally determined. The injection process can
be divided into developing phase and self-similar phase
according to the transient G. The effect of PR on the G
was not signiﬁcantly.
(4) The average equivalent ratio was large at the beginning
of injection under different PRs, and then decreased and
ﬁnally entered a stable range. The gas concentration
distribution was uneven along the axial direction. The
mole fraction was large near the nozzle hole and
decreased with the increase in Z
t, and entered a stable
range of 0.27 e0.37 when Zt was greater than 20 mm.
