ABSTRACT
The shock wave has become a common phenomenon in modern diesel engines with the continuous increase in
the fuel injection pressure. The shock wave re ﬂects in the cylinder and a ﬀects the macroscopic structure of the
spray and the mixing eﬀ ect between the spray and surrounding air. In this study, the eﬀ ects of the re ﬂected
shock wave on the evolution characteristics and the mixing characteristics of a high-pressure diesel spray were
investigated using the Schlieren imaging method. The results show that the re ﬂected shock wave signi ﬁcantly
suppresses the development of the spray in the radial direction. However, the re ﬂected shock wave has little
eﬀect on the development of the spray tip penetration. The volume and the average equivalence ratio of the
spray under the re ﬂected shock wave are also lower than those without the re ﬂected shock wave. In addition, the
evolution of the spray under di ﬀerent fuel injection angles was also analyzed. The results show that the di ﬀerent
fuel injection angle a ﬀects the development of the spray width in the radial direction, which in turn has a
signiﬁcant inﬂ uence on the spray cone angle.
1. Introduction
Increasing the fuel injection pressure can improve the atomization
of the spray and improve the combustion and emission performance of
the engine [1–3]. The velocity of the diesel spray at the nozzle exit also
increases with the increased fuel injection pressure. Shock waves are
induced when the spray reaches supersonic [4,5]. The propagation and
reﬂection of the shock waves in the cylinder a ﬀect the atomization of
the spray and the formation of the mixture, which a ﬀect the engine
performance and fuel economy [6–8].
With the continuous development of modern testing technology and
numerical simulation, studies related to these shock waves have been
reported [9–11]. Nakahira et al. ﬁrst observed the induced shock wave
structure and the shock wave re ﬂection during the fuel injection process
using the Schlieren imaging method. The results showed that the shock
wave behavior could a ﬀect the fuel jet breakup [9]. MacPhee et al.
studied the e ﬀect of the shock wave on the density ﬁeld in the cylinder
by an X-ray technique. The results showed that the shock wave could
lead to a 15% density increase [10]. Quan et al. numerically simulated
the supersonic diesel spray and the induced shock waves, and the si-
mulations were consistent with the experiments [11].
Further studies have focused on the e ﬀect of shock wave on the
development of the spray [12–15]. Payri et al. investigated the e ﬀect of
the shock wave on the spray macroscopic structures. The results showed
that the spray tip penetration in the shock-wave state is about 6%
higher than that in the non-shock-wave state [12]. Song et al. carried
out relevant research using the Schlieren imaging method and found
that the shock waves had a positive e ﬀect on the air entrainment into
the spray [13]. Im et al. investigated the e ﬀect of the shock wave on the
spray characteristics based on numerical simulations. The study found
that the shock waves have an impact on the formation of the air-fuel
mixture [14]. Huang et al. developed a modi ﬁed numerical model to
predict the spray tip penetration. The model could predict the spray
characteristics from the subsonic state to the supersonic state. This
analysis showed that the generation and propagation of shock waves
have a signi ﬁcant e ﬀect on the spray characteristics [15].
The shock wave can be divided into the attached leading shock
wave and the detached shock wave according to the relative position of
the shock wave and the spray front [17]. Recently, studies related to the
propagation
characteristics of the attached oblique shock wave have
been investigated [16–19]. Milton et al. investigated the leading shock
wave structure with a maximum injection pressure of 300 MPa. It was
found that the nozzle structure a ﬀected the shock wave structure [16].
A further study by Pianthong et al. found that the shock wave structure
was related to the spray Mach number and the nozzle geometry [17].

However, the propagation characteristic of the detached shock wave in

the Schlieren imaging method. This paper has three objectives: (1)
Study the e ﬀect of the re ﬂected shock wave on the macroscopic struc-
tures of the high-pressure diesel spray, (2) Analyze the inﬂ uence of the
reﬂected shock waves on the mixing characteristics of the air-fuel
mixture in the cylinder, (3) Understand the evolution of the spray at
diﬀerent fuel injection angles, and further investigate the in ﬂuence of
the re ﬂected shock wave on the spatial distribution of the spray.
2. Experimental setup
2.1. Experimental apparatus
As shown in Fig. 1 , the experimental setup consists of six parts: the
Schlieren system, fuel supply system, fuel injection system, control
system, monitoring system and a constant volume vessel. The frame
rate is set to 80,000 frames per second (fps) with a resolution of
80 * 184 pixels. The detailed experimental setup has been described in a
previous publication [13]. The experiment was carried out in a constant
volume vessel and a combustion chamber structure. The design of the
combustion chamber and the actual manufactured product are shown
in Fig. 2. The combustion chamber was installed in the constant volume
vessel. The combustion chamber structure was designed based on a
Yuchai diesel engine with the cylinder diameter of 129 mm. The re-
ﬂected shock waves are obtained when the detached bow shock wave
propagates forward and re ﬂects on the wall of the combustion chamber
structure. The combustion chamber can also achieve multi-degrees of
freedom adjustments in order to further analyze the impact of di ﬀerent
shock wave re ﬂection moments on the spray characteristic parameters.
2.2. Image processing and parameter de ﬁnition
An imaging processing program was developed in the MATLAB
environment to analyze the original Schlieren images and obtain the
spray macroscopic structure parameters. The program deals with the
visual images by adjusting the contrast and using small air erase tech-
nology. In this experiment, the real scale of a pixel was
351.6 μm × 351.6 μm. The extraction error of the spray tip penetration
is half the height of a pixel, which is 175.8 μm. The extraction error of
the spray cone angle is ± 1°. Each measurement condition was repeated
10 times in order to improve the accuracy of the results.

2.3. Experimental conditions
In this paper, SF 6 and N 2 were used as ambient gases to study the
eﬀect of the re ﬂected shock wave on the spray characteristics. This is
because the speed of sound in the SF 6 atmosphere is much lower than
that in the N 2 atmosphere. It can be seen that a large density gradient is
induced in the SF 6 atmosphere. Three fuel injection angles of 5°, 10°
and 15° were selected to investigate the e ﬀect of the di ﬀerent re ﬂected
shock waves on the evolution of the high-pressure diesel spray. In order
to clearly observe the re ﬂected shock waves, three injection pressures of
120 MPa, 140 MPa and 160 MPa were selected. The speci ﬁcations of the
experimental parameters are listed in Table 1.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. E ﬀects of re ﬂected shock wave on spray macroscopic structures and
mixing eﬀ ect
Fig. 4 shows the visual images of the evolution of the diesel spray
under the di ﬀerent diesel injection pressures ( Pinj), ambient pressure
(Pinj), fuel injection angle ( α) and gaseous medium. The re ﬂected shock
wave structure, the fuel spray structure and the induced shock wave can
be clearly observed from the ﬁgures. It is noted from Fig. 4(c) that the
reﬂected shock wave makes a second re ﬂection in the cylinder at about
0.2 ms after the start of the injection ( ASOI). As shown in Fig. 4 (e), the
shock waves and the re ﬂected shock waves in the SF 6 environment are
more obvious due to the low sound speed. In addition, the visual images
of the evolution of the spray without the re ﬂected shock waves have
been described in a previous publication [13]. The experiment was
carried out in the constant volume vessel without the combustion
chamber structure under the same fuel injection pressure and ambient
pressure. Therefore, the shock wave re ﬂection and non-re ﬂection are
realized by two di ﬀerent experimental structures. And the in ﬂuence of
the re ﬂected shock wave on the evolution of the spray is analyzed by
comparing the spray parameters at the same time.
3.1.1. Spray tip penetration
Spray tip penetration directly determines the contact area between
the spray and the surrounding air, and it is an important parameter to
evaluate the spatial distribution of the fuel. In this paper, the spray tip
penetration in the re ﬂected-shock-wave state and the non-re ﬂected-
shock-wave state are compared. The evolution of the macroscopic
structure parameters of the spray with no re ﬂected shock wave were
reported in a previous publication [13].
Fig. 5 shows a comparison of the spray tip penetration in the re-
ﬂected-shock-wave state and non-re ﬂected-shock-wave state. The fuel
injection pressure is 160 MPa in the N
2 environment when the ambient
pressure is 1 MPa, and the fuel injection pressure is 140 MPa in the SF 6
environment when the ambient pressure is 0.57 MPa. It can be seen
from Fig. 5 that the re ﬂected shock wave has little e ﬀect on the de-
velopment of the spray tip penetration. This is because the development
of the spray tip penetration can be divided into two stages. During the
ﬁrst stage, the re ﬂected shock wave does not contact the spray during
the initial stage of the spray development ( t
ASOI < 0.2 ms). So there is
almost no di ﬀerence between the curves. Then, the shock wave is re-
ﬂected from the wall and acts on the right interface of the spray body. It
can be seen from Fig. 4 that the propagation direction of the re ﬂected
shock wave and the spray axial direction are at an angle. Therefore, the
eﬀect of the re ﬂected shock waves on the development of the spray tip
penetration is still not obvious.

spray volume. The development of the spray in the radial direction is
suppressed during the early stage of the spray development due to the
eﬀect of the re ﬂected shock waves, and the development of the spray in
the axial direction undergoes no obvious change. This illustrates that
the mixing characteristics of the spray will be a ﬀected, and the fuel
spray cannot quickly entrain the surrounding air, which is a dis-
advantage for the combustion of the spray in the cylinder. The e ﬀect of
the reﬂected shock waves on the mixing characteristics of the spray will
be analyzed in the next section.

4. Conclusions
In this study, the e ﬀect of the re ﬂected shock wave on the evolution
of the high-pressure diesel spray is investigated using the Schlieren
imaging method. The main conclusions of this article are summarized
as follows:
(1). The e ﬀect of the re ﬂected shock wave on the macroscopic structure
of the spray was analyzed in N
2 and SF 6 gas atmospheres. The
reﬂected shock wave has an inhibiting e ﬀect on the development of
the spray in the radial direction. The spray cone angle in the re-
ﬂected-shock-wave state in the N 2 and SF 6 atmospheres is 1.85%
and 8.9% lower than that in the non-re ﬂected-shock-wave state,
respectively. However, the re ﬂected shock wave has little e ﬀect on
the spray tip penetration.
(2). The e ﬀect of the re ﬂected shock wave on the mixing characteristics
of the spray was analyzed. The re ﬂected shock wave has a negative
eﬀect on the air entrainment e ﬀect, which is not conducive to the
rapid mixing of the spray.
(3). The e ﬀect of di ﬀerent reﬂected shock waves on the deformation of
the spray interface is compared for the di ﬀerent fuel injection
angles. It was found that the di ﬀerent fuel injection angle changes
the development of the right side interface of the spray due to the
constant impact of the re ﬂected shock waves. However, the re-
ﬂected shock wave has little e ﬀect on the development of the spray
left interface. The spray has the maximum right interface width
when the fuel injection angle is 10°, which leads to the high spray
cone angle.
