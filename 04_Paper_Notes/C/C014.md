# C014

## 1. Bibliographic Identity

- Title: Effect of Mach number on droplet aerobreakup in shear stripping regime
- Paper ID / source record: C014 / C-SD-07
- Year / DOI: 2020 / 10.1007/s00348-020-03026-1
- Original PDF: `02_PDF_Raw/C/C-SD-07_2020_Wang_MachNumber_DropletAerobreakup_ShearStripping.pdf`

## 2. Pilot Role

Mach-number versus Weber-number benchmark for shock-induced droplet breakup.

## 3. Research Question

At approximately fixed Weber number, does post-shock flow Mach number independently modify deformation and breakup?

## 4. Study Type and Configuration

Shock-tube experiment on single water or ethylene-glycol droplets in air or CO2; high-speed shadowgraphy.

## 5. Geometry and Operating Conditions

24 m shock tube, 290 mm inner diameter; 190 × 190 mm test section; 100 kfps imaging at 0.087 mm/pixel.

## 6. Injection / Ambient Conditions

Post-shock uniform-flow Mach M∞ = 0.30–1.19, We ≈ 1050–1160, Re = 2.6×10^3–2.4×10^4. Uniform post-shock period ~1.6 ms.

## 7. Wave / Jet Structure

A planar incident shock establishes a post-shock gas flow. M∞ denotes that flow Mach, not incident-shock Mach.

## 8. Droplet / Spray Initial Conditions

Ethylene glycol: d0≈2.6 mm, Oh≈0.043; water: d0≈3.1 mm, Oh≈0.002. Single initially stationary droplets.

## 9. Dimensionless Parameters

We=ρg ug²d0/σ, Re=ρgugd0/μg, Oh=μd/√(ρdd0σ); gas quantities are post-shock. M∞ is post-shock freestream Mach.

## 10. Time Scales

T=t ug/d0 √(ρg/ρd). Authors note this normalization does not itself account for compressibility.

## 11. Main Physical Observations

At near-constant We, breakup changes from peripheral mist to multiple bags to leeward ligaments as M∞ rises; higher M∞ reduces flattening/sheet growth and delays breakup.

## 12. Quantitative Results

Eight tabulated cases preserve gas/liquid pair, M∞, We, Re, Oh, density ratio, and viscosity ratio separately.

## 13. Mechanism Proposed by Authors

Author interpretation: compressibility/shock-related flow structure modifies deformation and instability even when We is held near 1100; Re has a complementary but minor effect.

## 14. Wave → Droplet/Spray Response Chain

Planar shock → post-shock gas state and M∞ → aerodynamic loading at fixed We → deformation/sheet initiation → morphology and breakup timing.

## 15. Mixing / Ignition / Combustion Consequences

NA — nonreacting breakup.

## 16. Key Figures, Tables, and Equations

Definitions and time scaling pp.2–5; Table 1 p.5; morphology figures/results pp.7–15; conclusions p.15.

## 17. Reported vs Derived vs Inferred

Case values and morphologies are reported. Compressibility explanation is author interpretation. No plot digitization or project-derived values.

## 18. Limitations

We is only approximately matched; M∞, Re, density ratio, and gas identity covary. Shadowgraph gives projected morphology.

## 19. Evidence Candidates

- PILOT-C014-C01: At We near 1100, breakup morphology and timing still change substantially with post-shock freestream Mach number. [PDF p.15, Sec. 4 Conclusions]
- PILOT-C014-C02: The paper's Mach number is the post-shock freestream-flow Mach, whereas We uses post-shock gas density and velocity. [PDF p.5, Table 1 and Eqs. 1–3]

## 20. Contradictions / Comparison Hooks

Directly tests whether a generic Mach field and a scalar We are sufficient for cross-paper comparison.
