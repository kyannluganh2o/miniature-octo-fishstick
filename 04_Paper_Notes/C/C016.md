# C016

## 1. Bibliographic Identity

- Title: Shock induced aerobreakup of a droplet
- Paper ID / source record: C016 / C-SD-09
- Year / DOI: 2021 / 10.1017/jfm.2021.860
- Original PDF: `02_PDF_Raw/C/C-SD-09_2021_Sharma_ShockInduced_DropletAerobreakup.pdf`

## 2. Pilot Role

Core benchmark for the early wave-dominated stage and later aerodynamic breakup stage.

## 3. Research Question

How do incident-shock wave dynamics and the induced airflow sequence produce distinct breakup modes?

## 4. Study Type and Configuration

Shock-tube experiments with water droplets; synchronized shadowgraph/schlieren and top-view imaging.

## 5. Geometry and Operating Conditions

Exploding-wire shock generator; droplets d0 = 0.5, 2.5, 2.9 mm; incident shock Mach M_s = 1.1–1.8.

## 6. Injection / Ambient Conditions

We ≈ 30–15000 and Re ≈ 3000–110000; post-shock induced-airflow properties define We and Re.

## 7. Wave / Jet Structure

Stage I contains incident, reflected, transmitted and refracted/internal wave dynamics; Stage II is dominated by the induced airflow and surface instabilities.

## 8. Droplet / Spray Initial Conditions

Single water droplets; reported diameters 0.5, 2.5, and 2.9 mm; Oh from liquid properties and d0.

## 9. Dimensionless Parameters

M_s=V_s/c ahead of shock. We=ρ_a V_i²D_i/σ and Re=ρ_a V_iD_i/μ_a use post-shock induced-airflow density and velocity.

## 10. Time Scales

Paper separates wave-interaction Stage I from breakup Stage II; dimensionless imaging times are case-specific.

## 11. Main Physical Observations

Stage I is reported as self-similar across studied M_s. In Stage II, RTP and SIE modes depend on droplet diameter relative to the preferred KH wavelength, not We alone.

## 12. Quantitative Results

Representative cases span M_s=1.12–1.83, We=44–1.4×10^4, Re=2.5×10^3–1.3×10^5, with RTP or SIE labels.

## 13. Mechanism Proposed by Authors

Authors attribute SIE to KH instability and RTP to RT instability when KH development is unfavorable; the transition uses D_i versus λ_KH.

## 14. Wave → Droplet/Spray Response Chain

Incident shock and internal-wave focusing → short pressure redistribution → post-shock aerodynamic shear → KH/RT selection → sheet, ligaments, holes and recurrent breakup.

## 15. Mixing / Ignition / Combustion Consequences

NA — nonreacting water droplets.

## 16. Key Figures, Tables, and Equations

Definitions p.8; wave sequence p.11; representative cases/figures pp.13–15; conclusions p.29.

## 17. Reported vs Derived vs Inferred

Wave patterns and breakup modes are directly imaged; instability attribution and transition criterion are author interpretation/model-based.

## 18. Limitations

Finite imaging resolution; instability models are qualitative/order-of-magnitude; water only and discrete diameters.

## 19. Evidence Candidates

- PILOT-C016-C01: Shock-induced aerobreakup is described in two stages: early wave dynamics followed by airflow-driven breakup. [PDF p.29, Conclusions]
- PILOT-C016-C02: RTP can persist to We near 800 for small droplets, while SIE can occur near We 200 for larger droplets. [PDF p.13, Fig. 6]

## 20. Contradictions / Comparison Hooks
