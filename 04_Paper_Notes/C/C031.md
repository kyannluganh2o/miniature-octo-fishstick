# C031

## 1. Bibliographic Identity

- Title: Three-dimensional numerical simulations of phase change effects on shock-droplet interactions
- Paper ID / source record: C031 / C-PC-05
- Year / DOI: 2025 / 10.1063/5.0255860
- Original PDF: `02_PDF_Raw/C/C-PC-05_2025_Song_3D_PhaseChange_ShockDroplet_Interaction.pdf`

## 2. Pilot Role

3D hydrocarbon shock-droplet benchmark for real-fluid phase change, deformation, instability and mass flux.

## 3. Research Question

How do evaporation- and condensation-dominated interface states modify waves, shear, deformation and motion?

## 4. Study Type and Configuration

Three-dimensional conservative sharp-interface computation with real-fluid EOS and Hertz–Knudsen phase-change model.

## 5. Geometry and Operating Conditions

Quarter-domain symmetry; adaptive multi-resolution mesh; n-dodecane droplet D0=4.8 mm in n-dodecane vapor.

## 6. Injection / Ambient Conditions

Droplet 490 K; pre-shock vapor 500 K, 0.1 MPa; baseline incident shock Mach 1.47; post-shock vapor 510.49 K, 0.217 MPa, 123.45 m/s.

## 7. Wave / Jet Structure

Phase change adds an evaporation shock in vapor and rarefaction in liquid; classic reflected/transmitted waves and Mach reflection remain.

## 8. Droplet / Spray Initial Conditions

n-dodecane: ρ=593.5 kg/m³, μ=1.96×10^-4 Pa·s, σ=0.009 N/m as model inputs in Table II.

## 9. Dimensionless Parameters

Baseline We=8.25×10^4 and Oh=1.23×10^-3; We uses post-shock gas density/velocity and D0; t*=tug/D0√(ρg/ρl).

## 10. Time Scales

Early wave patterns are resolved at small t*; later deformation/circulation and mass-flux evolution are tracked. No single breakup completion time is claimed.

## 11. Main Physical Observations

Evaporation forms a cool vapor layer that lowers shear and suppresses KH waves; condensation strengthens shear and promotes flattening, sheet stretching and ligaments.

## 12. Quantitative Results

Model coefficients: evaporation-dominated k_e=0.4, k_c=0.03; condensation-dominated k_e=1.0, k_c=0.6. Mesh convergence beyond 256 cells/D0.

## 13. Mechanism Proposed by Authors

Simulation-resolved fields support phase-change → local thermodynamic layer → shear/vorticity → interface instability → deformation/motion coupling.

## 14. Wave → Droplet/Spray Response Chain

Shock and phase-change Riemann response → added waves and thermal layer → shear/circulation change → KH development → deformation, drag and mass evolution.

## 15. Mixing / Ignition / Combustion Consequences

No chemistry; high-temperature single-component vapor environment only.

## 16. Key Figures, Tables, and Equations

Phase model pp.5–6; definitions and grid p.7; Table II and baseline p.8; wave results pp.9–10; Mach effect pp.18–20.

## 17. Reported vs Derived vs Inferred

Material states and coefficients are reported model inputs; interface fields are simulation-resolved; causal phrasing follows author interpretation.

## 18. Limitations

Phase coefficients manually selected, not calibrated to shock-droplet phase-change statistics; early-stage emphasis; no reacting multicomponent environment.

## 19. Evidence Candidates

- PILOT-C031-C01: Evaporation can suppress KH-wave growth through a low-temperature vapor layer that reduces interfacial shear. [PDF p.20, Conclusions]
- PILOT-C031-C02: For the modeled condensation-dominated family, reducing shock Mach from 1.47 to 1.27–1.37 shifts net phase change toward evaporation. [PDF p.19, Fig. 16]

## 20. Contradictions / Comparison Hooks

Compare D017: C031 resolves interface phase change but not a detonation spray distribution; D017 measures spray survival but uses reduced models for interpretation.
