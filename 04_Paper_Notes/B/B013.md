# B013

## 1. Bibliographic Identity

- Title: Numerical investigation of a high pressure hydrogen jet of 82 MPa with adaptive mesh refinement: The starting transient evolution and Mach disk stabilization
- Paper ID / source record: B013 / B-H2-08
- Year / DOI: 2017 / 10.1016/j.ijhydene.2017.01.016
- Original PDF: `02_PDF_Raw/B/B-H2-08_2017_Tang_82MPa_HydrogenJet_AMR_MachDisk.pdf`

## 2. Pilot Role

Very-high-pressure starting-transient benchmark separating Mach-disk formation, trajectory, and stabilization.

## 3. Research Question

How does a nominal 82 MPa H2 source develop its near-field shock system from start-up to a stabilized Mach disk?

## 4. Study Type and Configuration

Three-dimensional compressible simulation with adaptive mesh refinement; nonreacting H2 jet into atmosphere.

## 5. Geometry and Operating Conditions

Single circular orifice d=0.2 mm; storage pressure 82 MPa; modeled sonic inlet p=43.2 MPa, T=249 K, u=1220 m/s.

## 6. Injection / Ambient Conditions

Ambient p=1 atm, T=300 K; reported NPR≈809.3 uses storage pressure/ambient, while the computational inlet pressure is lower.

## 7. Wave / Jet Structure

Initial compression system, overexpansion and secondary shock evolve into barrel shock and Mach disk; topology is explicitly transient.

## 8. Droplet / Spray Initial Conditions

NA — gas jet.

## 9. Dimensionless Parameters

Reported NPR 809.3; exit Mach 1 at inlet; local maximum Mach ~14 after expansion.

## 10. Time Scales

Mach disk forms around 19–22 μs and is described as dynamically stable around 20 μs; positions at 37–42 μs show small oscillation near 3.8 mm.

## 11. Main Physical Observations

Formation time is not interchangeable with later instantaneous or stabilized distance. A secondary shock originates after overexpansion below ambient pressure.

## 12. Quantitative Results

Nozzle d=0.2 mm; p0=82 MPa; pe=43.2 MPa; stabilized disk distance ~3.8 mm; finest AMR ~6 μm near key regions.

## 13. Mechanism Proposed by Authors

Simulation-resolved shock adjustment produces the barrel/Mach-disk system; proposed Reynolds-similarity explanation for formation timing is speculative.

## 14. Wave → Droplet/Spray Response Chain

Valve/inlet start → expansion and overexpansion → secondary shock → Mach-disk formation → axial motion/oscillation → quasi-steady position.

## 15. Mixing / Ignition / Combustion Consequences

NA — nonreacting.

## 16. Key Figures, Tables, and Equations

Boundary conditions Table 1 p.5; transient results pp.11–12; conclusions p.14.

## 17. Reported vs Derived vs Inferred

Numerical fields and stated times are reported; Reynolds-similarity discussion is author hypothesis.

## 18. Limitations

Idealized inlet boundary; storage and sonic-inlet pressures are distinct; no measured injector filling transient.

## 19. Evidence Candidates

- PILOT-B013-C01: Mach-disk formation time, later instantaneous position, and stabilized position are distinct observables. [PDF p.12, Figs. 17–18 and discussion]
- PILOT-B013-C02: The nominal NPR based on 82 MPa storage pressure does not uniquely encode the 43.2 MPa sonic-inlet boundary. [PDF p.5, Table 1]

## 20. Contradictions / Comparison Hooks
