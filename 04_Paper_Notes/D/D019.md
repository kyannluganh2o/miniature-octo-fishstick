# D019

## 1. Bibliographic Identity

- Title: Comparative analysis of detonation and shock waves interacting with droplets: Characteristics and mechanisms
- Paper ID / source record: D019 / D-DW-04
- Original PDF: `02_PDF_Raw/D/D-DW-04_2026_Zou_DetonationShock_DropletInteraction_Mechanisms.pdf`

## 2. Tier 1 Role

Tier 1 comparative application evidence for reaction-heat-release effects on wave, cavitation and breakup mechanisms.

## 3. Research Question

How do equal-leading-strength detonation and inert shock waves differ in reflected waves, cavitation timescales and droplet deformation?

## 4. Study Type and Configuration

2D multicomponent compressible two-phase simulation with chemical-potential phase transition and two-step detonation chemistry.

## 5. Geometry and Operating Conditions

Planar wave impacting a 4.8 mm water droplet; 5 um grid selected; 2D configuration is treated as an upper-bound estimate of interaction intensity.

## 6. Injection / Ambient Conditions

Stoichiometric H2/O2 with 70% Ar at 300 K and 50 kPa; inert shock modeled as zero-reaction detonation with equal leading-shock Mach 4.8.

## 7. Wave / Jet Structure

Early topology is similar, but postwave pressure/temperature/velocity states change reflected-shock propagation and low-pressure persistence.

## 8. Droplet / Spray Initial Conditions

Water droplet. Phase transition is enabled for cavitation analysis and disabled only for uncluttered wave-topology comparison.

## 9. Dimensionless Parameters

Reported We/Re differ strongly between detonation and shock cases; source does not atomize all component definitions, so NV components are retained.

## 10. Time Scales

Thermal conduction O(1e2 s) versus deformation O(1e-4 s) motivates omission for the main droplet; vapor history is figure-only.

## 11. Main Physical Observations

Detonation accelerates reflected shocks, shortens/asymmetrizes cavitation collapse, and produces leeward flattening rather than shock-case RT forward jetting.

## 12. Quantitative Results

Table I gives We/Re; mesh and timescale values are localized; no figure values were digitized.

## 13. Mechanism Proposed by Authors

Reaction heat release changes postwave sound speed and velocity; pressure attenuation shortens low-pressure duration; postwave velocity controls recirculation and RT response.

## 14. Wave → Droplet/Spray Response Chain

Wave type/heat release -> postwave state -> reflected/expansion wave loading -> cavitation and vortex/instability response -> deformation/breakup.

## 15. Mixing / Ignition / Combustion Consequences

Application relevance is to detonation propulsion; transfer to HPDI/shock-droplet review is comparative agent synthesis, not an author claim about engines.

## 16. Key Figures, Tables, and Equations

Setup/Table I pp.3-4; method pp.4-7; mesh pp.7-8; wave comparison pp.10-15; cavitation pp.16-20; deformation pp.20-24; conclusions p.24.

## 17. Reported vs Derived vs Inferred

Mach, We/Re, geometry and timescales are reported. Project-level transfer implications are explicitly separated from reported conclusions.

## 18. Limitations

2D geometry, idealized water, no viscosity/surface tension, limited microstructure/child-droplet fidelity, and no multidroplet/practical-fuel analysis.

## 19. Evidence Candidates

T1-D019-C01 to C03 retain wave-speed, cavitation and breakup-mechanism differences.

## 20. Contradictions / Comparison Hooks

Compare detonation and shock only at matched leading-shock definition; distinguish postwave velocity, pressure persistence, chemistry and dimensionality.

## 21. Parameter-Schema Gaps

None blocking. Source-limited We/Re component definitions are represented with NV rather than invented properties.

## 22. Relevance to Review Mainline

Adds a controlled comparative bridge from shock loading to detonation application evidence.
