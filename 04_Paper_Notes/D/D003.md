# D003

## 1. Bibliographic Identity

- Title: Potential of a jet-induced shock wave to influence an upstream droplet cloud in compression-ignition engines using multiple injection strategies
- Paper ID / source record: D003 / D-IC-03
- Year / DOI: 2018 / 10.1016/j.ijmultiphaseflow.2018.01.001
- Original PDF: `02_PDF_Raw/D/D-IC-03_2018_Tetrault_JetInducedShockWave_UpstreamDropletCloud.pdf`

## 2. Pilot Role

Core loading-duration test for a jet-induced shock followed closely by an expansion wave.

## 3. Research Question

Can a weak jet-induced shock and its short post-front flow accelerate an upstream droplet cloud during multi-injection operation?

## 4. Study Type and Configuration

Schlieren/dynamic-pressure experiments plus a one-dimensional dilute two-phase transient model.

## 5. Geometry and Operating Conditions

Five-hole diesel injector, 120 μm orifices; injection pressure differential 1600 bar; nitrogen vessel at room temperature.

## 6. Injection / Ambient Conditions

Ambient pressure 1–30 bar in dynamic measurements; shock amplitude gives M≈1.0005–1.1; spray–shock spacing includes ~40 mm observations.

## 7. Wave / Jet Structure

Jet-emitted compression/shock wave is followed closely by an expansion wave, producing a thin shocked region rather than sustained post-shock flow.

## 8. Droplet / Spray Initial Conditions

Modeled cloud density 0.003–24 kg/m³, thickness 1.5–4.5 mm, droplets 0.05–50 μm; breakup rate set to zero.

## 9. Dimensionless Parameters

Shock Mach inferred from pressure ratio with Rankine–Hugoniot relation. No droplet We-based breakup analysis is reported.

## 10. Time Scales

Shock forms around 30 mm at ~148 μs after SOI; shock-to-spray delay ~115 μs at 40 mm. Shocked-state exposure is ~60–100 μs, distinct from front passage and later response.

## 11. Main Physical Observations

Increasing cloud loading attenuates shock strength and induced velocity. Smaller droplets extract more momentum and respond within the short shocked-state interval.

## 12. Quantitative Results

Reference model: ambient 2 MPa, 293 K, pressure amplitude ratio 1.085, cloud density 6 kg/m³. For 50 μm droplets, shocked-state ~60 μs versus Stokes response ~750 μs.

## 13. Mechanism Proposed by Authors

Model-based: push-away arises from momentum transfer during a short, expansion-terminated shocked region; inertia limits large-droplet response.

## 14. Wave → Droplet/Spray Response Chain

Injector event → weak compression/shock front → finite shocked gas layer → expansion wave → transient slip/drag → cloud acceleration; breakup excluded.

## 15. Mixing / Ignition / Combustion Consequences

Non-evaporating, nonreacting vessel; engine-relevance is a proposed mechanism context.

## 16. Key Figures, Tables, and Equations

Experimental conditions pp.2–3; shock amplitude p.5; model assumptions pp.5–8; response/scales p.9; conclusions p.10.

## 17. Reported vs Derived vs Inferred

Shock timing and pressure are measured; droplet response is model-resolved; push-away relevance is author interpretation.

## 18. Limitations

One-dimensional model, dilute cloud, breakup and phase change disabled, averaged forcing, simplified multi-hole wave interaction.

## 19. Evidence Candidates

- PILOT-D003-C01: A jet-induced shock can create a push-away impulse, but the effective shocked-state duration is shortened by a following expansion wave. [PDF p.10, Conclusions]
- PILOT-D003-C02: Droplet response depends strongly on particle size relative to the short forcing interval. [PDF p.9, Figs. 12–13 and discussion]

## 20. Contradictions / Comparison Hooks

Shows that front passage, pressure pulse, post-wave flow, expansion wave, and total forcing interval are not interchangeable.
