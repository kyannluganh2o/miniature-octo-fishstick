# D017

## 1. Bibliographic Identity

- Title: Droplet breakup and evaporation in liquid-fueled detonations
- Paper ID / source record: D017 / D-DW-02
- Year / DOI: 2025 / 10.1016/j.expthermflusci.2024.111324
- Original PDF: `02_PDF_Raw/D/D-DW-02_2025_Young_DropletBreakup_Evaporation_LiquidFueledDetonations.pdf`

## 2. Pilot Role

Micron-droplet detonation benchmark for concurrent breakup/evaporation and canonical-model transfer limits.

## 3. Research Question

What survival distance and cloud morphology result when small n-dodecane droplets experience a self-sustained detonation?

## 4. Study Type and Configuration

Liquid-fueled detonation experiment with PDPA initial characterization and planar Mie-scattering snapshots; reduced 0D model comparison.

## 5. Geometry and Operating Conditions

Vertical 57×57 mm square detonation tube; n-dodecane droplets in oxygen; imaging resolution 12.1 μm/pixel.

## 6. Injection / Ambient Conditions

Equivalence ratio 0.62±0.09; mean wave speed 1897.46±59.80 m/s; model post-shock gas 2288 K, 5.07 MPa, 1796 m/s.

## 7. Wave / Jet Structure

Self-sustained multiphase detonation; measured wave-speed deficit ~10% relative to equivalent gaseous CJ calculation.

## 8. Droplet / Spray Initial Conditions

D10=5.5 μm, D30=12.6 μm, D32=40.7 μm, D43=89.6 μm; most count below 20 μm but mass weighted toward larger droplets.

## 9. Dimensionless Parameters

We=ρg u_g²d_p/σ with post-shock relative velocity; characteristic We spans O(10^4–10^5) for the measured distribution.

## 10. Time Scales

Observed liquid-survival region 10.88±0.52 mm behind the front. Models treat concurrent deformation, repeated breakup and evaporation with 1 ns step.

## 11. Main Physical Observations

Breakup clouds are oblate and lateral to flow. Evaporation alone predicts survival orders longer; breakup sharply increases surface area and vaporization.

## 12. Quantitative Results

No-breakup >500 mm for ≥40 μm; KH-RT predicts ≤2.97 mm through 120 μm; WERT49 predicts 0.075–11.96 mm for 1–120 μm.

## 13. Mechanism Proposed by Authors

Model-based interpretation: tiny KH children evaporate nearly immediately; larger RT-scale children persist and spread while evaporating.

## 14. Wave → Droplet/Spray Response Chain

Detonation front/variable post-front acceleration → parent deformation and breakup → child-cloud area growth → heat/mass transfer → vapor availability and wave-speed deficit.

## 15. Mixing / Ignition / Combustion Consequences

Reactive n-dodecane/oxygen detonation; reduced droplet model holds gas state constant and has no reaction coupling.

## 16. Key Figures, Tables, and Equations

Distribution Table 2 p.4; facility/method p.5; survival p.6; models pp.7–9; conclusions p.10.

## 17. Reported vs Derived vs Inferred

Clouds and survival are observed; KH/RT child-size partition is model-based; transferability to canonical shock-tube models is a project inference.

## 18. Limitations

Single-time images across repeated shots; Mie intensity not direct size/count; reduced models assume constant post-shock conditions and are uncalibrated at high We/variable acceleration.

## 19. Evidence Candidates

- PILOT-D017-C01: Evaporation alone cannot explain the observed ~10.9 mm droplet-survival region; concurrent breakup is required in the model interpretation. [PDF p.9, Table 4]
- PILOT-D017-C02: Existing shock-driven breakup models predict faster extinction than some observed droplets under the detonation's variable acceleration. [PDF p.10, Conclusions]

## 20. Contradictions / Comparison Hooks

Canonical shock-driven models bracket but do not fully reproduce detonation observations; preserve this as a synthesis candidate, not reported result.

G005, G010, G011, G016

## 22. Relevance to Review Mainline

Tests joint storage of breakup, evaporation, variable acceleration, cloud morphology and model validity.
