# A007

## 1. Bibliographic Identity

- Title: Influence of the Spatial and Temporal Interaction Between Diesel Pilot and Directly Injected Natural Gas Jet on Ignition and Combustion Characteristics
- Paper ID / source record: A007 / A-NG-07
- Original PDF: `02_PDF_Raw/A/A-NG-07_2018_Fink_DieselPilot_NGJet_SpatialTemporalInteraction.pdf`

## 2. Tier 1 Role

Primary Tier 1 chronology and interaction pillar for pilot-ignited HPDI natural-gas combustion.

## 3. Research Question

How do relative injection timing and jet angle jointly control pilot ignition, transfer to the gas jet, and heat-release shape?

## 4. Study Type and Configuration

Primary RCEM experiment with separate single-hole injectors, shadowgraphy, pressure/position measurements, and a multizone heat-loss model for HRR.

## 5. Geometry and Operating Conditions

200 mm bore, 14 dm3 RCEM; jet angle varied from -90 to +30 deg; injector origins offset by about 5 mm.

## 6. Injection / Ambient Conditions

TDC 75 +/- 1.5 bar and 780 +/- 4 K. Diesel: 2000 bar, 3 mg, 395 us, SOI -2 ms ATDC. Gas: 330 bar, 80 mg, 2730 us; gas SOI -2.5 to +1.5 ms relative to pilot.

## 7. Wave / Jet Structure

No underexpanded-jet shock topology is quantified; shadowgraphy resolves jet/spray overlap and density-gradient structures.

## 8. Droplet / Spray Initial Conditions

Diesel pilot and natural-gas jet; no droplet-size distribution is reported.

## 9. Dimensionless Parameters

No comparison-ready NPR, We, Re, Oh, or Mach value is reported; pressure bases are not assumed.

## 10. Time Scales

Pilot and gas SOI, ignition thresholds, and SOI separation are typed. HRR history is figure-only and not digitized.

## 11. Main Physical Observations

Strong interaction can delay or prevent pilot ignition, but proper overlap promotes gas ignition. Gas-ignition timing relative to SOI organizes HRR shape.

## 12. Quantitative Results

Ignition becomes possible below about +25 deg between diverging jets; tested timing/angle ranges and injection conditions are retained.

## 13. Mechanism Proposed by Authors

Authors attribute pilot deterioration to gas-driven dilution or isolation from air entrainment, local cooling, and possibly altered kinetics; gas ignition benefits from contact with burned pilot products.

## 14. Wave → Droplet/Spray Response Chain

Timing and angle set overlap -> overlap modifies pilot mixture/ignition -> burned pilot region ignites gas -> ignition relative to gas SOI sets premixing -> premixing shapes HRR.

## 15. Mixing / Ignition / Combustion Consequences

The pilot/gas ignition trade-off constrains combustion stability and controllable premixing; +10 to +20 deg is proposed as a robust compromise.

## 18. Limitations

RCEM lacks swirl, walls and adjacent jets of an engine; hot-product heat losses are neglected, so HRR supports qualitative comparisons rather than burnout completeness.
